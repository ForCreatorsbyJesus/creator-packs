# Prepare and stream

## Inputs

- For `go_live_soon`: time until live, total time available, stream duration, platform, topic, objective, established format, setup, support, and reuse goal
- For `plan_week`: weekly creator-hours, sustainable live and publishing cadence, fixed commitments, recovery needs, primary objective, platform roles, backlog, and team capacity

## Imminent stream procedure (`go_live_soon`)

1. Choose one concept that fits the creator's voice and primary objective. Present it as the default, not a guaranteed winner.
2. Build a Tonight's Stream Packet: concept; title and thumbnail direction; opening hook; timed run of show; audience prompts and CTA; technical and moderation preflight; fallback; planned clip moments; active experiment and success signal; post-stream capture.
3. Treat the stated total as a hard ceiling across preparation, live time, breaks, capture, and shutdown. Reserve a small buffer before adding polish.
4. In rapid 90-minute mode, protect only essentials: 10 minutes for decision, 20 for run of show and clip beats, 20 for technical/moderation checks, 10 for hook and packaging, 10 for contingencies, and 20 as buffer or creator reset. Shorten these blocks when the 90 minutes belongs to a larger total-time ceiling.
5. Assign platform roles explicitly. Let livestream interaction serve the live platform; use clear story beats and capture notes for later YouTube use without forcing an unfamiliar format.
6. Provide fallbacks for absent face-cam, local recording, multitrack audio, moderator, or thumbnail assets.

## Weekly planning procedure (`plan_week`)

1. Set one weekly objective and choose a sustainable cadence from the creator's confirmed capacity, not an idealized posting frequency.
2. Allocate the full weekly creator-hours across live creation, preparation, repurposing, audience/community work, review/admin, and explicit recovery or buffer. Include recurring setup, export, and handoff time.
3. Give each platform one role, such as live relationship, long-form depth, short-form discovery, or community follow-up. Reuse source material where useful without requiring every stream to serve every platform.
4. Sequence dependencies and protect the highest-value block first. Limit work in progress; defer or drop artifacts that exceed the weekly ceiling.
5. Mark one active experiment, one primary success signal, and one review point. Keep the experiment small enough to evaluate within the creator's cadence.
6. Build a weekly creator plan containing objective, time allocation, cadence, platform roles, scheduled work blocks, recovery/buffer, experiment, deferred work, and one default action to start.

## Output contract

- For `go_live_soon`, return a completed Tonight's Stream Packet, a budget check showing all work fits, one labeled experiment with one primary success signal, and one next action.
- For `plan_week`, return a weekly creator plan with a complete creator-hours allocation, sustainable cadence, cross-platform roles, explicit recovery/buffer, one experiment and review point, deferred work, and one next action.

## Graceful degradation

- For `go_live_soon`, if format or equipment is unknown, preserve the existing format, avoid equipment-dependent instructions, and give a low-tech fallback such as spoken transitions plus a timestamp note.
- For `plan_week`, if weekly hours or cadence is unknown, propose a minimum viable one-cycle plan with labeled assumptions, keep at least one unallocated buffer block, and ask for the single capacity fact that would most change the plan.

## Example

For a ceramic artist with six creator-hours this week, plan one 90-minute live demonstration, 60 minutes of preparation, 75 minutes for one edited technique recap, 30 minutes for community follow-up, 30 minutes for review/admin, and 75 minutes of recovery/buffer. Let Twitch handle live questions and YouTube hold the durable technique recap; defer Shorts this cycle.
