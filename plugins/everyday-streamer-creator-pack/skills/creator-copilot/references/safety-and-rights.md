# Safety and rights

## Inputs

- Proposed content, source materials, people shown or named, and target platform
- Known permissions, licenses, sponsorships, affiliate relationships, and creator guardrails
- Workload or well-being concerns relevant to the request

## Procedure

1. Minimize personal data. Remove or obscure addresses, legal names, private messages, account details, location clues, minors' identifiers, and accidental screen or voice disclosures unless clearly authorized and necessary.
2. Identify disclosure needs for sponsorships, gifts, affiliate links, synthetic or materially altered media, and other relevant relationships. Use current official platform or local guidance when exact wording matters.
3. Screen third-party music, video, images, chat, game footage, trademarks, and clips for copyright, privacy, and permission risk. Distinguish ownership, license, permission, exception, and platform policy; do not treat credit as clearance.
4. Use no-clearance language: “Rights or permission are not confirmed; keep this out of the publish-ready draft or replace it with cleared material.” Never claim legal clearance.
5. Respect the creator's guardrails and well-being. Do not recommend unsafe stunts, harassment bait, doxxing risk, deceptive urgency, or a schedule that erases breaks and recovery.
6. Prepare a review checklist or safer substitute. Never publish or submit on the creator's behalf.

## Output contract

Return flagged item, risk basis, evidence or unknown status, safer substitute, creator review step, and one next action. Keep uncertain legal or policy points explicitly uncertain.

## Graceful degradation

When rights, consent, jurisdiction, or disclosure status is unknown, do not guess. Exclude the item from a publish-ready draft, offer a replace/trim/mute/blur option, and recommend confirmation from the rights holder, current official policy, or qualified counsel as appropriate.

## Example

The VOD contains background music with no supplied license. Mark rights as unconfirmed, mute or replace that segment in the edit brief, and ask the creator to verify the license before any upload. Do not say that attribution makes use safe.
