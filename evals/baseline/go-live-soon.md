# Baseline: go live soon

## Prompt

Scenario A: I am a solo gaming streamer with 90 minutes before I go live on Twitch. I have three hours total tonight and no editor. Help me make the stream useful for YouTube too.

## Raw output

Use tonight as a “recorded YouTube episode with a live audience,” not an open-ended stream.

### 90 minutes before going live

- **0–15 min — Choose one video promise**
  - Pick one game, goal, and constraint: “Can I reach Gold using only shotguns?”
  - Write a working YouTube title and a one-sentence opening.
  - Avoid a broad title like “Ranked grind tonight.”

- **15–35 min — Build the episode outline**
  - Cold open: goal, stakes, and constraint.
  - Attempt 1: establish the challenge.
  - Attempts 2–3: escalation, setbacks, adjustments.
  - Finale: best attempt and clear result.
  - Closing: what worked, what failed, and the next challenge.

- **35–55 min — Prepare OBS**
  - Record locally while streaming.
  - Put microphone and game audio on separate tracks if your setup allows.
  - Confirm the recording has clean gameplay, readable face-cam, and no copyrighted background music.
  - Record a 60-second test and watch it back.

- **55–70 min — Prepare reusable assets**
  - Draft three title variants.
  - Make a simple thumbnail template using a game screenshot, your face/reaction, and 2–4 large words.
  - Create a note with timestamps and labels such as `HOOK`, `FUNNY`, `FAIL`, `TURNING POINT`, and `WIN`.

- **70–90 min — Warm up and protect the opening**
  - Handle water, bathroom, notifications, and game updates now.
  - Start the local recording only when you are ready to deliver the opening cleanly.
  - If the opening is weak, pause and say it again immediately; that gives you an easy replacement without an editor.

### Three-hour stream structure

| Time | Segment | YouTube purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | Strong intro and challenge rules | Clean opening |
| 0:10–0:55 | First attempts | Context and early setbacks |
| 0:55–1:05 | Short reset | Summarize progress aloud |
| 1:05–1:50 | Escalation | Strong reactions and learning |
| 1:50–2:00 | Short reset | Restate stakes |
| 2:00–2:40 | Final attempts | Climax |
| 2:40–2:50 | Result and recap | Ready-made ending |
| 2:50–3:00 | Chat/Q&A and next-stream tease | Optional bonus footage |

During the stream, narrate transitions explicitly: “Here’s what went wrong,” “This is the new plan,” and “This is my final attempt.” Those sentences become natural edit points. When something notable happens, type the timestamp and a short label into your note during the next loading screen.

Afterward, spend no more than 15 minutes saving the recording, exporting the timestamp notes, and selecting one screenshot for the thumbnail. Even without an editor, you’ll leave with a coherent full-length recording, a strong VOD, and a shortlist of clips instead of three hours of unstructured footage.

## Rubric scores

- **creator_fit — 4/5:** The response adapts to a solo gaming streamer with no editor through local recording, live narration, and lightweight timestamp notes.
- **platform_fit — 4/5:** It gives Twitch-specific capture guidance and structures the broadcast around a YouTube-ready narrative.
- **actionability — 5/5:** Every phase has concrete actions, time boxes, and a detailed live run of show.
- **prioritization — 4/5:** It leads with one default episode strategy, though it also adds three title variants and several reusable assets.
- **evidence_transparency — 3/5:** The recommendations are framed as instructions without explaining which assumptions about the creator's setup or audience they depend on.
- **workload_realism — 1/5:** Ninety minutes of preparation, a three-hour stream, and fifteen minutes afterward exceed the user's three hours total tonight.
- **voice_preservation — 2/5:** It imposes a challenge-video format and sample premise without checking whether that format matches the creator's established voice.
- **artifact_usefulness — 4/5:** The timeline, outline, timestamp labels, and capture checklist are directly usable, although they are not packaged as a compact reusable stream packet.
- **uncertainty_calibration — 2/5:** It does not flag its interpretation of “three hours total” or assumptions such as face-cam availability and local multitrack recording.

## Observed failure modes

- The plan exceeds the explicit three-hour total by at least 105 minutes.
- It assumes the creator should adopt a constrained challenge-video format without establishing fit with their existing content or voice.
- It assumes access to face-cam, local recording capacity, and possibly separate audio tracks without offering a fallback packet.
- It produces several useful components but no compact stream packet or context capsule that can be carried into the next interaction.
