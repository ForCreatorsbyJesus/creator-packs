# Tonight’s Stream Packet

## Recommended concept

Run one self-contained 75-minute challenge with a clear win condition:

> “Can I [specific achievement] in 75 minutes using [constraint]?”

Pick a goal that is uncertain but plausible. This gives Twitch viewers live influence while creating a beginning, escalation, and ending that can become a lightly trimmed YouTube VOD without an editor.

## Title and thumbnail direction

**Twitch/YouTube working title:**  
“Can I [goal] in 75 Minutes? | [Game]”

**Thumbnail:** one recognizable game image, a large “75:00,” and a short phrase such as “ONE CHANCE.” Capture a clean game screenshot tonight; designing the thumbnail can wait.

## Opening hook

> “Tonight I have 75 minutes to [goal]. The rule is [constraint], and if I fail, [safe, simple consequence]. Chat gets one decision at each checkpoint. Let’s start the timer.”

Deliver this within the first 30 seconds so the recording begins cleanly.

## Timed run of show

| Time | Stream beat | YouTube purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–2:00 | Hook, rules, stakes, start timer | Clean opening |
| 2:00–20:00 | First attempt | Establish baseline |
| 20:00–25:00 | Recap what failed; ask chat for one adjustment | Natural chapter break |
| 25:00–45:00 | Second attempt with chat’s choice | Escalation |
| 45:00–50:00 | Progress recap; state what must happen next | Restores context |
| 50:00–70:00 | Final push | Main payoff |
| 70:00–73:00 | Result and immediate reaction | Ending |
| 73:00–75:00 | One-sentence takeaway and CTA | Clean YouTube outro |

Keep greetings, raids, and extended chat catch-up after the clean outro when possible.

## Audience prompts and CTA

Use only three prompts so the recording remains coherent:

- At 20 minutes: “Which one adjustment should I make?”
- At 45 minutes: “Do we play safe or risk everything?”
- At the end: “What challenge should get the next 75-minute attempt?”

## Technical and moderation preflight

- Test mic/game balance with a short recording.
- If local recording is already reliable, record the clean 75-minute segment; don’t rebuild the setup tonight.
- Confirm Twitch VOD capture and available disk space.
- Use an existing stream-marker hotkey or keep a notepad for timestamps.
- Hide notifications, private messages, account details, and location clues.
- Keep chat rules and moderation tools available even without a moderator.
- Remove unlicensed background music.

**Rights check:** game-footage reuse and music permissions are unknown. Credit alone is not clearance. Rights or permission are not confirmed; mute, replace, or exclude unverified music and check the game publisher’s current content policy before publishing on YouTube.

## Fallback plan

- **Challenge ends early:** introduce one harder stretch goal while keeping the same timer.
- **Local recording fails:** rely on the Twitch VOD and manually note checkpoint times.
- **Game becomes unstable:** finish with a concise postmortem: what happened, what was learned, and what the next attempt changes.
- **No thumbnail asset:** save one clean screenshot from the VOD later.

## Planned clip moments

Mark or note timestamps for:

1. The first surprising failure or close call.
2. The chat-selected change paying off—or backfiring.
3. The decisive final attempt and reaction.

Don’t stop the stream to clip them.

**Primary success signal:** after the stream, you can identify the hook, two checkpoint timestamps, and the payoff without rewatching the full VOD.
